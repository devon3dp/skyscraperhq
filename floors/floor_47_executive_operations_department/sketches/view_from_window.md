# View From the Window

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

The window runs the length of the east wall, single pane, brass-framed, surprisingly quiet — at this height the wind hums the cables more than the glass.

On a clear morning the view is mostly other rooftops. F58's gardens are the obvious one — pale stone terraces, low cypress, a stretch of grass kept green by something none of us are quite sure about. Below them, F49 Studio shows its skylights; below those, the long copper roof of F41 with its weather-stained OANDA terrace railings. Further down, the lift cables fall away in three parallel lines, and every minute or so a car slides past, sealed packet inside, going about its business.

This afternoon the clouds are low. F58's gardens are hidden. The cables disappear into white about two floors below us and reappear nowhere — you take it on faith that they meet the lobby. Visitors find this troubling. The decorators don't; they like how the brass frame holds a square of pure cloud, as if the building were issued its own private weather.

At night the view inverts. The rooftops vanish; the cables become threads of reflected light from cabin lamps; and far above, just visible if you press your forehead to the glass, the Penthouse holds its soft amber glow. The Penthouse is empty — reserved for a kernel that has not been built — but the lamp is left on. Wren says a skyscraper that goes dark at the top looks abandoned. So the lamp stays.

The window remembers nothing. Each visitor is given the view as it is.
