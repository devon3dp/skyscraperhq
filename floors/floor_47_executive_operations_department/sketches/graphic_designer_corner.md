# Graphic Designer's Corner

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

The Corner is the smallest room on the studio side — barely larger than the desk in it. A single angle-poise lamp, a glass of water, a stack of small index cards, a pen tray with three weights of pen. The graphic designer sits with her back to the wall, face to the window. She prefers natural light, so most pills and badges are drawn between mid-morning and three.

What she does is small in size and large in reach. Every pill, glyph, badge or label the tower wears was first sketched on one of those index cards. Visitors will recognise her work without knowing it:

- The brass-and-paper **directory button** at the top of the cockpit. She drew its glyph at three sizes before settling on the 18px survivor.
- The discreet **welcome pill** that sits beside the header on Open Day and links visitors out to the warm welcome page.
- The **Open Day tagline** in the header strip, with its three dot-separators carefully spaced.
- The four **status pills** beside the header. Each starts as a skeleton-shimmering "loading…" pill and is overwritten when the real state ticks through.
- A second family entirely: the ticker filter pills and the readiness pills the cockpit borrows from.

And on the welcome page: the quiet single-line tagline. She drew it three ways and chose the one that read most softly.

If you ask, she will hand you a card and a pen and let you sketch one of your own. She keeps the good ones in a small wooden box under the desk. Some day, she says, a future designer will reach in, pull one out, and use it.
