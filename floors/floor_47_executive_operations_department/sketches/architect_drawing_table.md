# Architect's Drawing Table

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

Turn left out of the lift on Floor 47 and the first room on the studio side is the Architect's Drawing Table. A long oak surface, three pendant lamps in a row above, two stools at the south end. The rest is kept clear for paper, tracing vellum and the heavy brass T-square the senior architect uses to keep horizontals honest.

This is where the building's structural moves begin. The crew here do not patch cockpit JS or tune a colour; their work is at the scale of "should we open the north wall for a window?" or "can the lift lobby be widened for two arrivals at once?" Every proposal that touches the *shape* of the tower starts on this table.

Visitors are welcome to look at what is currently pinned up. At time of writing the table holds three open sketches:

1. **North-window panel reorganisation** — a study of moving the south-wall radiator panel to free up the north wall for daylight.
2. **Lift-lobby widening** — accommodating two simultaneous guests at peak hours on Open Day.
3. **Kettle Corner relocation** — moving the Break Area away from the working studios so off-duty hum doesn't carry into the Atelier.

None of these have been signed off. None have been built. The table is a thinking space, not a doing space — sketches travel from here to the Workshop Bench, then to the Sandbox Lab, then to the Sign-Off Desk before anything is touched. Until then they are only pencil on paper, kept tidy by people who care about the building.

If you ask politely, the architect on duty will show you the brass T-square. It belonged, she says, to "an earlier Wren."
