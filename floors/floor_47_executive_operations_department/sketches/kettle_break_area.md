# Kettle & Break Area

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

The break area sits in the south-west corner of the floor, where the corridor bends away from the workshop bench and the sign-off desk. You find it by accident the first time. Most operatives find it by the sound: a low, almost-not-there ticking of an old brass-cased wall clock, and once every few minutes the soft click of a kettle resetting itself.

There are two armchairs. They are mismatched on purpose — one deep green wool, one cracked tan leather — because the decorators decided early that a break room with matching chairs would feel like a waiting room, and a waiting room is the opposite of what is wanted here. Between them is a small round table with a kettle on a wooden board, two heavy mugs, a tin of loose tea, a jar of instant coffee, and a folded cloth. Nothing more.

The window faces west. In the afternoon the light falls across the leather chair first and then, slowly, across the rug. Visitors who sit here often go quiet. The clock ticks. The kettle stays warm. Outside, several floors below, the tower goes on doing its work.

What this room *is*, in the building's accounting, is a deliberate non-operational space. It has no daemon, no audit jsonl, no endpoint. The descriptor lists it as `operational_status: non-operational` and `visitor_open_hours: always`, and the kettle's `current_kettle_state` is hard-coded to `warm` — not because someone is monitoring the element, but because the building has decided that, for the crew, the kettle is always warm.

It is the only room on Floor 47 whose entire purpose is to remind the operatives — Wren, the F47 fleet, the visitors who stop by — that rest is permitted. That sitting in an armchair with a mug, watching the light cross the rug, is not idleness. It is part of the work.

The clock ticks. The two chairs wait.
