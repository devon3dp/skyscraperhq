# Decorator's Atelier

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

The Atelier sits two doors down from the Architect's Drawing Table. Where architects work in pencil on vellum, decorators work on swatch cards, fabric off-cuts and small jars of pigment under a low brass lamp. The room smells faintly of beeswax. By reputation, it is the most pleasant room on the floor.

What the decorators do is mostly invisible to visitors — which is exactly the point. They tune the surfaces of the building. Two palettes are kept on the wall in matched frames:

- **The Welcome palette** (warm, for guests). `--bg-deep` is the near-black of polished walnut. `--brass`, `--brass-bright` and `--brass-dim` are the three weights of the fittings. `--ivory` is the paper-cream of the welcome card. `--warm-grey` and `--line` hold the quieter surfaces together; `--accent` is the orange ember reserved for moments of warmth.
- **The Cockpit palette** (cool, for operators). `--bg` and `--panel` are the deep-navy of an operations room at night. `--gold`, `--green`, `--blue` are the three signal weights. Where Welcome warms a guest, Cockpit calms a working operator.

The roster lists twenty-five operatives across five trades — colorist, decorator, graphic designer, scene fitter, ui designer. None act on their own. Every change travels the same route as a structural sketch: bench, sandbox, sign-off, applier. The Atelier holds the *intent* of a change; the building only wears it once the floor agrees.

On a quiet afternoon you may find the lead decorator with a single swatch card, holding it up against the brass lamp, turning it slowly. She is not idle — she is asking whether the cream is too cold for a welcome card, or whether the gold of a cockpit pill has begun to feel tired. Surfaces age. The Atelier remembers.
