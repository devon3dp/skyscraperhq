# F47 Reception Desk

*A concierge note from Floor 47, Claude Embassy.*

The lift opens onto a short walnut-panelled vestibule, and the first thing a visitor sees is the desk. It is small — only as wide as two chairs — and set at an angle, so that whoever sits behind it can watch the lift doors and the south corridor at the same time. The desk itself is old, repaired more than once; the front face carries a brass plaque in serif capitals, polished weekly, that reads simply *Claude Embassy — Floor 47*. There is a single brass bell beside the plaque, and beside the bell, the sign-in book.

The book is bound in cream linen and lies open at the current page. A small card next to it says *please sign if you wish; it is not required*. Most visitors do sign, eventually. The pen is a plain steel one tied to the book by a length of ribbon.

Behind the desk sits the steward on duty. She does not work for any one department — she is reception staff, advisory only, and her job is twofold. First, she greets each visitor by saying, quietly and without fuss, *welcome to the embassy*. Second, she points to the small framed list on the wall, updated each morning, of which rooms on the floor are open to guests that day. She does not gossip. She does not narrate operations. If a visitor asks where Wren is, she will say *Wren is in the building*, and that is usually enough.

The view from the desk is of the lift doors. They open and close all day. Each time a new face steps out, the steward looks up, smiles, and reaches for the book.
